## Hints

- Which metrics measure speed, and which measure stability? Do the two really trade off?
- Why is "lines of code" or "story points per sprint" a poor measure of delivery?
- What happens when a metric becomes a target for individual performance reviews?

## Answer

The **DORA metrics** (from Google's DevOps Research and Assessment program and the book *Accelerate*) measure **software delivery performance** at the team or service level. The classic four:

**Throughput:**
- **Deployment frequency:** how often you successfully release to production.
- **Lead time for changes:** time from commit to running in production.

**Stability:**
- **Change failure rate:** the share of deployments that cause a failure needing a fix, rollback or hotfix.
- **Failed deployment recovery time** (formerly MTTR): how long it takes to restore service after a bad change.

Recent DORA reports also track **rework rate** (unplanned deployments made to fix user-facing issues) and pair the metrics with reliability, using SLOs.

**Key finding:** speed and stability are **not** a trade-off. Elite teams deploy on demand with lead times under a day *and* have low failure rates. Small batches, trunk-based development, strong test automation, and fast rollback drive both.

**Measuring in practice:**
- Deploy events from the CD tool, commit and merge timestamps from Git, incidents from the incident tracker, linked by commit SHA or release ID.
- Tools: the open-source Four Keys approach, built-in dashboards in GitHub, GitLab or engineering-insights platforms, or a simple script over deploy logs.

**Using them well:**
- Use them for **team improvement and trends**, never to rank individuals (Goodhart's law: people game the target).
- Look at them together. High deploy frequency with a rising failure rate is a warning.
- Combine them with developer experience signals (SPACE framework, surveys) and business outcomes.

**Follow-up:** "Your lead time is two weeks. Where would you look?" Break it down into time waiting for review, CI duration, waiting for a release window, and manual QA, then fix the biggest wait first.
