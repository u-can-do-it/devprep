## Hints

- What was done first: finding the root cause or restoring service?
- How was communication handled during the incident, both inside the team and toward users?
- What is a "blameless" postmortem, and which concrete follow-ups came out of it?

## Answer

This tests how you behave under real pressure, and whether you and your team **learn systemically** rather than blaming people.

**Structure your story:**
1. **Situation:** what broke, user impact, how it was detected (alert, customer report) and how long it lasted.
2. **Your role:** responder, incident lead, communicator, or the person whose change caused it. Owning that is a plus.
3. **Response:**
   - **Mitigate first:** roll back, disable a feature flag, scale up, fail over. Restore service before full root-cause analysis.
   - **Coordinate:** a clear incident lead, a dedicated channel, status updates to stakeholders and a status page, and a timeline written as you go.
   - **Investigate** with data: dashboards, traces, logs, recent deploys.
4. **Resolution:** the root cause and contributing factors (rarely just one).
5. **Postmortem:** a **blameless** review that focuses on *how the system allowed this* (missing test, no canary, a confusing alert, an unsafe default) instead of *who* made the mistake. Output: owned, tracked action items: an alert on the right symptom, automated rollback, a guardrail in CI, an updated runbook.
6. **Outcome:** what changed and whether it prevented recurrence.

**Concepts worth naming:** detection, mitigation and recovery times (MTTD/MTTR), SLOs and error budgets, runbooks, on-call rotation, and the "five whys" (used carefully, since incidents usually have several causes).

**What interviewers look for:** calm prioritization, ownership, clear communication, a learning culture, and follow-through on action items. Senior candidates should show they improved the *process*.

**Red flags:**
- Blaming a colleague or another team.
- Debugging for hours while users are down instead of rolling back.
- No postmortem, or action items that were never done.
- Claiming you've never been involved in an incident (at senior level).
