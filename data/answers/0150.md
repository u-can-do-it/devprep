## Hints

- What research should you do before naming a number?
- Is it better to give a single figure or a range — and where should your target sit in that range?
- What do your questions to the interviewer reveal about you?

## Answer

**Salary expectations**

The interviewer wants to know whether you fit the budget and whether your expectations are realistic.

- **Research first:** market data for the role, level, and location (salary surveys, levels.fyi-type sites, recruiters, peers). Know your floor.
- If asked early, you can ask for their budget range first: "Could you share the range for this role?" — in many places (e.g. EU pay transparency rules, several US states) companies must disclose it.
- If you give a number, give a **researched range** with your target near the bottom of it, and mention it depends on the full package (equity, bonus, benefits, remote work).
- Clarify gross vs net, and employment vs contract (B2B).

**Red flags:** no idea of market rates, an unjustified number, or lying about your current salary.

**Questions to ask the interviewer**

Always have some — "No questions" signals low interest. Good questions reveal what you care about:

- "What does success look like in the first 3–6 months?"
- "How does the team ship code — review process, CI/CD, release frequency?"
- "What's the biggest technical challenge the team faces right now?"
- "How are technical decisions made, and how is on-call handled?"
- "How do you support growth — mentoring, career ladder, learning budget?"
- "Why is this position open?"

Tailor them to the interviewer (an engineer vs a manager vs HR), and avoid asking things easily found on the website. Leave benefits and vacation details for the offer stage or HR.
