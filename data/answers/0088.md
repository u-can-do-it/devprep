## Hints

- Is the related data always read together with the parent, and how many items can there be?
- What happens to a document if an embedded array grows without bound? (MongoDB caps documents at 16 MB.)
- If the embedded data is shared and changes, how many documents must you update?

## Answer

In document databases you **design for your queries**, not for normalization. The core decision is whether related data lives inside the parent document or in a separate collection referenced by id.

**Embed when:**

- The data is **read together** with the parent most of the time.
- It's a **one-to-one or one-to-few** relationship with bounded size (addresses, order line items).
- The child has no meaning on its own and is owned by the parent.
- You want **atomic updates** on a single document (no multi-document transaction needed).

**Reference when:**

- The relationship is **one-to-many with unbounded growth** (comments on a viral post, events, logs) – risk of hitting the 16 MB limit and slow, growing documents.
- **Many-to-many** relationships (students ↔ courses).
- The child is **shared and updated independently** (a product referenced by many orders), so embedding would duplicate data and require fan-out updates.
- The child is often queried on its own.

```json
{
  "_id": "order_123",
  "customerId": "cust_9",
  "items": [
    { "productId": "p_1", "name": "Keyboard", "priceAtPurchase": 79.0, "qty": 1 }
  ],
  "shipping": { "city": "Kraków", "zip": "30-001" }
}
```

Here line items are embedded (bounded, owned, read together), the customer is referenced, and product `name`/`price` are **deliberately denormalized snapshots** (historical correctness).

Hybrid patterns: **extended reference** (store id plus a few frequently read fields), **subset pattern** (embed the latest 10 reviews, keep all in a separate collection), **bucket pattern** for time-series. `$lookup` exists but heavy use usually signals relational data.
