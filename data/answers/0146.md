## Hints

- Are you moving *towards* something or only running *away* from something?
- How can you be honest without criticizing your current employer?
- How does your answer connect to what this company offers?

## Answer

The interviewer is checking your motivation, whether the same issue will make you leave them too, and how professionally you talk about others.

**Structure:**

1. A brief, honest reason, framed positively (one or two sentences).
2. What you're looking for next — growth, scope, technology, domain, team size.
3. Why this role matches that.

Example shape: "I've learned a lot in three years there, especially on the backend. The product is now in maintenance mode, and I want to work on something growing fast where I can take more ownership of architecture — which is why this role caught my attention."

**Acceptable reasons:** growth or learning, wanting different tech or domain, bigger impact or scope, company direction changed, relocation or remote work, layoffs (just state it factually — it's common and not a red flag).

**What interviewers look for:**

- Positive, forward-looking framing.
- Consistency with your CV and other answers.
- Realistic expectations that this company can actually meet.

**Red flags:**

- Badmouthing a manager, team, or company — even if it's true.
- Money as the only motivation (it's fine as one factor, not the whole story).
- Vague or evasive answers that sound like something is hidden.
- Reasons that will also apply at this company (e.g. "too many meetings" when you're applying for a lead role).

**If you're unemployed or a junior without a job,** reframe: "What are you looking for in your next role?"
