## Hints

- What becomes harder when a function call turns into a network call?
- How does team size and the number of teams affect the choice?
- Is there a middle ground between one deployable and dozens of services?

## Answer

**Monolith:** one deployable application, usually one database.

- Simple to develop, test, deploy, and debug; in-process calls are fast; transactions are easy.
- But as it grows: slow builds, tight coupling, all-or-nothing deployments, one team's bug can take everything down, and you scale the whole app even if only one part is hot.

**Microservices:** many small services, each owning a business capability and its own data, deployed independently and communicating over the network (HTTP/gRPC, events).

- Independent deployment and scaling, team autonomy, fault isolation, freedom to choose tech per service.
- But you pay the **distributed systems tax**: network latency and failures, eventual consistency instead of transactions (sagas), distributed tracing, service discovery, versioned contracts, much more infrastructure and DevOps maturity required.

**When to pick:**

- Start with a **modular monolith** for new products and small teams — clear module boundaries inside one codebase.
- Extract services when there's a concrete reason: many teams blocked on each other, parts with very different scaling or reliability needs, or a clearly bounded domain.

The worst outcome is a **distributed monolith**: services that must be deployed together and share a database — all the costs, none of the benefits.

**Follow-up:** "How would you migrate a monolith?" — strangler fig pattern: route specific endpoints to new services one at a time.
