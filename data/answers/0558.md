## Hints

- What did you notice that nobody had asked you to fix, and how did you know it was worth fixing?
- How did you get time and buy-in without a ticket?
- Did you finish it, and can you show the result in numbers?

## Answer

**What the interviewer is testing:** ownership (you act on problems you see instead of waiting), **judgement** (you pick problems that matter, not pet projects), finishing (initiatives that end in production, not abandoned branches), and communication (you bring people along instead of going rogue).

**Good kinds of stories:**

- A slow query, a recurring error or a flaky test that everyone had learned to ignore.
- A manual process that ate hours every week and you automated (a script, a CI job, an n8n workflow, an agent).
- Developer or agent tooling: faster CI, a local environment that starts in one command, a repo set up for coding agents, an MCP server for an internal system.
- A gap in monitoring that you found and closed before it caused an incident.

**Structure (STAR, with numbers):**

1. **Situation:** what you noticed and how (data, an alert, your own frustration, a support channel).
2. **Sizing:** why it mattered: time lost, errors, money, risk. "Support spent about 6 hours a week copying cancelled appointments into a spreadsheet."
3. **Action:**
   - **Validate:** check with the owner that it's a real problem and that nobody else is fixing it.
   - **Prove it small:** a proof of concept in a day or two, or time-boxed slack time.
   - **Get buy-in:** "I can remove this manual work in two days; OK to take it next sprint?"
   - **Ship incrementally** and hand it over: docs, an owner, monitoring.
4. **Result:** "The job runs every night; support stopped doing it by hand, and we found two bugs in the cancellation flow along the way."
5. **Reflection:** what you learned, and what you'd do differently (for example, involve support earlier).

**Example answer, short:** "Our CI took 25 minutes and people batched changes to avoid it. Nobody owned it. I measured where the time went: two thirds was installing dependencies and running the full end-to-end suite on every PR. I proposed caching and running end-to-end tests only for affected apps, built it over two afternoons behind an opt-in label, showed the numbers in the team meeting and rolled it out. CI went down to 8 minutes, and PRs got smaller. Afterwards I added a dashboard so we'd notice if it crept up again."

**Pitfalls:**

- Hero stories that bypass the team or skip review.
- Initiatives that were really personal preference ("I rewrote it in a new framework").
- Unfinished projects.
- No numbers.
- Blaming others for not having noticed.

**Prepare two or three stories** of different sizes: a small fix, a tool or automation, and something that changed how the team works.

**Follow-up:** tell me about an initiative of yours that failed or that you abandoned. Why, and what did you learn? (Shows judgement: knowing when to stop matters as much as starting.)
