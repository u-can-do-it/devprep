## Hints

- Who decides *what* gets built, and who decides *how*?
- Is backlog refinement one of the Scrum events?
- What do story points estimate, and why not hours?

## Answer

**Scrum** is a framework for delivering work in short, fixed iterations called **Sprints** (usually 1–4 weeks, most often 2), producing a usable increment each time and adapting based on feedback.

**Three accountabilities** (the 2020 Scrum Guide's word for roles):

- **Product Owner:** maximises the product's value. Owns and orders the **Product Backlog**, sets the Product Goal, decides *what* is built and in which order.
- **Scrum Master:** helps the team use Scrum effectively, removes impediments, coaches. Not a project manager.
- **Developers:** everyone building the increment (frontend, backend, QA, design). They decide *how* to do the work and own the Sprint plan.

**Five events:**

- **The Sprint** itself, containing all the others.
- **Sprint Planning:** why this Sprint matters (the **Sprint Goal**), what can be done, and how.
- **Daily Scrum:** 15 minutes for Developers to inspect progress towards the Sprint Goal and adapt the plan. Not a status report to a manager.
- **Sprint Review:** demo the increment to stakeholders, gather feedback, update the backlog.
- **Sprint Retrospective:** improve how the team works: what went well, what didn't, one or two concrete actions.

**Three artifacts**, each with a commitment: **Product Backlog** (Product Goal), **Sprint Backlog** (Sprint Goal), **Increment** (**Definition of Done**: tested, reviewed, accessible, deployed or deployable).

**Backlog refinement** is an ongoing activity, not an official event: splitting stories, clarifying acceptance criteria and estimating, usually a session or two per Sprint.

**Estimation:**

- **Story points** estimate relative effort, complexity and uncertainty compared to reference stories, not hours, because people are better at comparing than at predicting time, and points don't vary with who does the task.
- **Planning poker:** everyone reveals an estimate at once (Fibonacci-like 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13). Big differences start a useful discussion about hidden complexity.
- **Velocity** (points completed per Sprint) helps forecast; it isn't a productivity metric and isn't comparable between teams.
- Alternatives: t-shirt sizes for rough roadmap planning, or counting stories of similar size (#NoEstimates).

**Kanban** is the common alternative: continuous flow, no Sprints, **WIP limits** per column, measured by cycle time and throughput. Many teams mix the two ("Scrumban").

**Good answers in interviews** mention practical experience: what made your retros useful, how you handled scope added mid-Sprint, or what happened when estimates were wrong.

**Follow-up:** halfway through a Sprint the Product Owner wants an urgent new feature. How should the team handle it?
