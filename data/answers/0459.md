## Hints

- How small can a clickable target be under WCAG 2.2 AA?
- Which success criterion was removed in 2.2, and why?
- Since June 2025, does an online shop or a bank in the EU have a legal duty to be accessible?

## Answer

**WCAG 2.2** (W3C Recommendation, October 2023) is backwards compatible with 2.1: everything that passes 2.2 passes 2.1. It adds **9 success criteria** and removes one.

New at level **A/AA** (the usual legal target):

- **2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured (Minimum), AA:** the focused element must not be completely hidden by sticky headers, cookie banners or chat widgets. Fix with `scroll-padding-top` and by not covering content.
- **2.5.7 Dragging Movements, AA:** anything done by dragging (sortable lists, sliders, maps) needs a single-pointer alternative such as buttons or a menu.
- **2.5.8 Target Size (Minimum), AA:** pointer targets at least **24×24 CSS px**, or enough spacing around them (with exceptions for inline links).
- **3.2.6 Consistent Help, A:** help mechanisms (contact, chat, FAQ) appear in the same place across pages.
- **3.3.7 Redundant Entry, A:** don't make users re-type information they already gave in the same process; auto-fill it or let them select it.
- **3.3.8 Accessible Authentication (Minimum), AA:** no cognitive function test (memorising, transcribing, puzzles) to log in unless there's an alternative. Allow paste and password managers, support passkeys and email links. Simple object-recognition CAPTCHAs are allowed at AA.

New at **AAA:** 2.4.12 Focus Not Obscured (Enhanced), 2.4.13 Focus Appearance, 3.3.9 Accessible Authentication (Enhanced).

**Removed:** 4.1.1 Parsing. Browsers and assistive technology now handle malformed HTML consistently, so it no longer affects users.

**European Accessibility Act (EAA, Directive 2019/882)** has applied since **28 June 2025**. It makes accessibility a legal requirement for many **private-sector** products and services sold in the EU, including:

- e-commerce, **banking services** (online and mobile banking, ATMs), e-books, passenger transport ticketing and information, electronic communications, and the websites and apps that deliver them.
- Microenterprises providing services (fewer than 10 staff and at most €2M turnover) are exempt.
- The technical benchmark is the harmonised standard **EN 301 549**, which references **WCAG 2.1 AA**; its update is aligning with 2.2. Many teams target 2.2 AA now to be future-proof.
- Providers must publish information on how the service meets the requirements (an accessibility statement). National market-surveillance authorities enforce it with penalties. Poland implemented it with a 2024 act on accessibility of certain products and services.

**In practice:** add 2.2 checks to your definition of done (target sizes, sticky elements vs focus, drag alternatives, login flow), and remember that automated tools catch only part of WCAG, so add keyboard and screen-reader testing.

**Follow-up:** your login has a "type the characters from the image" CAPTCHA and blocks paste in the password field. Which criteria does it fail, and what would you replace it with?
