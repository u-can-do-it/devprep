## Hints

- Health data is a special category under GDPR. What does that change compared with an email address?
- Where does patient data end up besides the database: logs, traces, error reports, analytics, emails, LLM prompts?
- A patient asks you to delete their data, but the law requires keeping medical records for years. Who wins?

## Answer

**Legal frame (be able to name it, not recite it):**

- Under GDPR, **health data is a special category** (Art. 9): processing is forbidden unless an exception applies, such as providing healthcare or explicit consent.
- Large-scale processing needs a **DPIA**; every vendor that touches the data (cloud, monitoring, email/SMS, an LLM provider) is a processor and needs a **data processing agreement**, and the data should stay in the EU.
- A breach must be reported to the regulator within **72 hours**.
- Medical records also fall under national law. In Poland documentation is generally kept for 20 years, so the **right to erasure doesn't apply** to it (GDPR allows exceptions for legal obligations), while marketing data and accounts without records can be deleted.
- In the US the equivalent is HIPAA.

**Technical measures:**

- **Know your data:** classify fields (identity data such as PESEL, contact data, health data: symptoms, diagnoses, prescriptions, notes, recordings) and minimise: don't collect or copy what you don't need.
- **Encryption:** TLS everywhere, including service-to-service and database connections; encryption at rest (managed by the cloud, optionally with customer-managed keys); **field-level envelope encryption** with a KMS for the most sensitive fields, which also allows crypto-shredding (delete the key, the data is unreadable).
- **Access control:** least privilege; a doctor sees only their own patients' records (object-level checks, #371, #379); support tools show masked data; **break-glass** access with a reason and an alert; just-in-time production access with no shared accounts.
- **Audit logs:** who read or changed which patient's record and when, append-only, retained and reviewed. "Who looked at my data?" is a question patients and auditors ask.
- **Keep it out of side channels**, where most leaks really happen:
  - **Logs:** log ids, not content. Prefer an allowlist of fields over a denylist, and add redaction as a safety net.
  - **APM and error tracking:** obfuscate SQL parameters and request bodies, scrub span attributes and error contexts, use a sensitive-data scanner.
  - **URLs:** they end up in access logs, analytics and `Referer` headers, so no diagnosis or PESEL in paths or query strings.
  - **Analytics and session replay:** no health data in event properties; mask inputs.
  - **Notifications:** "You have a new message from your doctor", not the content; push notifications appear on lock screens.
  - **Non-production environments:** synthetic or properly anonymised data. Pseudonymised data is still personal data.
  - **The browser:** don't keep health data in `localStorage`; set `Cache-Control: no-store` on sensitive responses.

**AI and agents:**

- Sending patient data to an LLM is a transfer to a processor: you need a DPA, an EU or approved region, no-training and limited-retention terms, and a legal basis. De-identify before the prompt where you can.
- MCP servers and agent tools for logs, traces and databases (#542) must **not return patient data** to a coding agent: redact or aggregate at the tool layer, keep them read-only, and never give agents production database credentials.
- Patient-entered text is untrusted input to an LLM (prompt injection, #481).

**Process:** threat modelling for new features (#376), privacy review in the definition of done, retention rules per data type with deletion jobs (backups expire by rotation), dependency and secret scanning, penetration tests, and an incident playbook that includes the 72-hour notification. Healthcare is also an essential sector under NIS2, which adds security and incident-reporting duties.

**Follow-up:** support wants to paste chat transcripts with patients into an LLM to draft answers. What do you need before saying yes? (A legal basis and DPA, an approved provider and region, de-identification, no training on the data, access control and logging, a human approving every answer, and a DPIA update.)
