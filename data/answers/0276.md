## Hints

- Is `https://app.example.com` the same origin as `https://api.example.com`? As `http://app.example.com`?
- If any page could read responses from your bank's site using your cookies, what would go wrong?
- Can a page *display* a cross-origin image, even if it can't *read* it?

## Answer

The **same-origin policy (SOP)** is the browser's core security boundary. A document or script from one **origin** can't **read** data belonging to another origin.

**An origin = scheme + host + port.** All three must match exactly:

| Compared with `https://app.example.com` | Same origin? |
|---|---|
| `https://app.example.com/other/page` | Yes (the path doesn't matter) |
| `http://app.example.com` | No (scheme) |
| `https://api.example.com` | No (host; subdomains are different origins) |
| `https://app.example.com:8443` | No (port) |

**What SOP blocks (reads):**

- Reading responses from cross-origin `fetch`/XHR, unless the server allows it with **CORS** headers.
- Accessing another origin's DOM through iframes or `window.open` (only `postMessage` and a few properties such as `location` assignment work).
- Accessing another origin's `localStorage`, IndexedDB and cookies through JavaScript.
- Reading pixels from a cross-origin image drawn on a canvas (the canvas becomes "tainted").

**What it generally allows (embedding and writes):**

- Embedding resources: `<img>`, `<script src>`, `<link rel="stylesheet">`, `<video>`, `<iframe>`.
- Navigating and **submitting forms** to other origins. That's why **CSRF** is possible, and why it's defended with `SameSite` cookies and tokens, not by SOP.

**Why it matters:** without SOP, any site you visit could call `fetch('https://yourbank.com/account')`. The browser would attach your cookies, and the malicious page could read the reply.

**Related terms:**

- **Site** (scheme + registrable domain, e.g. `example.com`) is a looser grouping used by `SameSite` cookies. Origin is stricter.
- `document.domain` relaxation is deprecated and being removed.
- **CORS** is the controlled way to relax SOP for reading. **CSP** and **COOP/COEP** tighten things further.

**Follow-up:** SOP is enforced by the **browser**, not the server. Curl or a backend can call any API, so SOP never replaces server-side authorization.
