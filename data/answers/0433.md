## Hints

- What was your first internal reaction, and what did you actually do next?
- How did you check whether the feedback was valid?
- What concrete change can you point to afterwards?

## Answer

This question tests **coachability and emotional maturity**: can people give you hard feedback without it becoming a battle?

**Structure:**
1. **Context:** who gave the feedback (lead, peer, code reviewer, user) and what it was about. Keep it specific and real, e.g. PRs too big to review, unclear communication in meetings, or a design that ignored operational concerns.
2. **Your reaction:** it's fine to admit it stung. What matters is that you **listened, didn't get defensive, and asked clarifying questions** ("Can you show me an example? What would good look like?").
3. **Action:** what you changed, concretely: splitting PRs into stacked changes, writing a short design doc before coding, asking for an early review.
4. **Result:** evidence of improvement (faster reviews, the same person noticing the change, fewer incidents) and ideally **closing the loop** by asking for follow-up feedback.
5. **Learning:** how you now *seek* feedback proactively.

If you partly disagreed, show how you handled it: you acknowledged the valid part, explained your view with data, and agreed on a way forward.

**What interviewers look for:** a growth mindset, humility, the ability to separate ego from work, and turning feedback into action. For senior roles, also how you use feedback to improve team practices.

**Red flags:**
- "I've never really received critical feedback."
- Feedback that was trivial or secretly praise.
- A story where the person giving feedback was simply wrong and you were right.
- No visible behavior change afterwards.
