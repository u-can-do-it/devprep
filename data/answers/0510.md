## Hints

- When is the cheapest moment to raise a problem with a design?
- How do you make it about users and evidence rather than your personal taste?
- What do you do if the designer disagrees and the deadline is close?

## Answer

The interviewer wants to see that you **review designs critically but collaboratively**: early, backed by evidence, with a proposed solution, and respecting that design decisions belong to the designer.

**What to check in a Figma design before you implement it:**

- **Missing states:** loading, empty, error, disabled, hover/focus/active, long text, very large numbers, 0 or 1,000 items
- **Responsiveness:** what happens between the breakpoints that were drawn? Tables on mobile?
- **Accessibility:** color contrast, information conveyed by color alone (red/green), visible focus, target size of at least 24×24 px (WCAG 2.2), labels instead of placeholders, error messages
- **Consistency:** does it use existing design-system components and tokens, or a near-copy of one ("a 13px button that's almost our secondary button")?
- **Real data:** long names, other currencies and locales, missing fields, slow APIs

**How to raise it:**

1. **Early:** at refinement or design review, not after the sprint. A comment in Figma costs minutes, while a rework costs days.
2. **Concretely:** a screenshot, a short comment, and the rule or user impact ("fails 4.5:1 contrast", "a keyboard user can't reach this", "our design system already has a pattern for this").
3. **With options:** "We could use the existing `Tabs` component, or I can build this variant and we add it to the library."
4. **Respecting ownership:** the designer and PO decide. If you still disagree, agree on how to decide (a quick test with users, checking analytics, an accessibility audit).
5. **Under deadline pressure:** ship the agreed version, log the issue as a ticket, and follow up. Don't silently implement something different from the design.

**Example answer in STAR format:**

> **Situation:** We were building a transfers history page for a client portal. The design showed payment status only as a red or green dot, and a 9-column table on mobile.
>
> **Task:** I was implementing the page and noticed both issues while estimating it.
>
> **Action:** Before the sprint started, I left Figma comments with a contrast-checker screenshot and a note that color alone fails WCAG 1.4.1. I proposed using our design system's `StatusBadge` (icon plus text) and a card layout below 768px, and mocked the card version quickly in Storybook so we could compare.
>
> **Result:** The designer adopted the badge everywhere and added the card pattern to the design system, so other teams reused it. QA found no accessibility issues on that page in the audit, and we agreed I'd join design reviews for upcoming features.

Use your own real story, but keep this structure: you noticed it early, gave evidence, offered a solution, and the outcome benefited beyond one screen.
