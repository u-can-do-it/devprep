## Hints

- How strict is the schema in each, and who enforces data integrity?
- What happens to joins and multi-row transactions when data is spread across many nodes?
- Does "NoSQL" mean one kind of database, or several different models?

## Answer

> SQL databases give you a fixed schema, constraints, flexible queries with JOINs and strong ACID transactions. NoSQL is a family of models (document, key-value, wide-column, graph) that usually scale out more easily and let the schema evolve, but you design around known access patterns and give up rich joins and sometimes strong consistency. SQL is the sensible default for relational data like orders and payments. Many systems use both, and "it scales" alone is not a good reason to pick NoSQL.

**SQL (relational)** – PostgreSQL, MySQL, SQL Server:

- Fixed schema, enforced constraints (FK, unique, check).
- Powerful ad-hoc queries, JOINs, aggregations.
- Strong **ACID** transactions across tables.
- Scales vertically easily; horizontal write scaling needs sharding/partitioning (or distributed SQL like CockroachDB, Spanner).

**NoSQL** is a family of models:

- **Document** (MongoDB, Firestore) – JSON-like docs, flexible schema, data shaped like the app objects.
- **Key-value** (Redis, DynamoDB) – very fast lookups by key.
- **Wide-column** (Cassandra, ScyllaDB) – huge write throughput, partitioned by key.
- **Graph** (Neo4j) – relationship-heavy traversals.

Typical NoSQL trade-offs: easier **horizontal scaling** and high availability, schemas that evolve without migrations, but you **model around access patterns**, joins are limited or absent, and consistency is often tunable/eventual (CAP trade-offs), though MongoDB and DynamoDB do support transactions now.

**Choosing:**

- Relational, consistent data with varied queries (orders, payments, users) → SQL. It's the sensible default.
- Massive scale with known access patterns, flexible/nested data, or specialized needs (caching, time series, graph) → the matching NoSQL store.
- Many systems use both (polyglot persistence): Postgres as the source of truth plus Redis for caching, Elasticsearch for search.

Modern blur: Postgres `JSONB` covers many "need flexible documents" cases.

Red flag in interviews: choosing NoSQL only "because it scales" or "no schema", without discussing access patterns and consistency needs.
