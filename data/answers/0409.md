## Hints

- What failure does spreading servers across zones protect you from, and what failure doesn't it?
- Why might you not want to replicate a database synchronously across continents?
- Can legal requirements decide where your data lives?

## Answer

> Cloud providers split their infrastructure so one failure doesn't take everything down. A region is a geographic area, and each region has several availability zones: separate data centers with their own power and network. Running across two or three zones is the normal baseline for production. Multiple regions protect against a whole region failing, but they're much harder because replication between them is usually asynchronous. Users far from your region also see higher latency. Make sure every dependency, including the database, is multi-zone too.

- A **region** is a geographic area (e.g. `eu-central-1` Frankfurt, `us-east-1` Virginia) containing several isolated data centers.
- An **availability zone (AZ)** is one or more physically separate data centers within a region, with independent power, cooling and networking, linked by low-latency connections (single-digit milliseconds).

**Availability:**
- **Multi-AZ** is the baseline for production: run app instances in at least 2–3 AZs behind a load balancer, and use a database with a synchronous standby in another AZ (e.g. RDS Multi-AZ). That survives a data center outage with little or no data loss.
- **Multi-region** protects against a whole region failing (rare but it happens) and provides disaster recovery. It's much harder: cross-region replication is usually **asynchronous** (some data loss is possible), you need failover routing (DNS or global load balancers), and you have to handle data conflicts. Define **RTO** (how fast you recover) and **RPO** (how much data you can lose) before choosing.

**Latency:** users far from the region pay round-trip time on every request (Europe to US East is roughly 80–100 ms). Mitigations: a CDN or edge for static and cacheable content, read replicas closer to users, or active-active multi-region for global apps.

**Other factors:**
- **Data residency** (GDPR, local laws) may require keeping data in a specific region.
- Prices and service availability differ per region.
- Cross-AZ and cross-region **data transfer costs** money.

**Pitfall:** "multi-AZ" app servers that all depend on a single-AZ database or a single NAT gateway. Check every dependency.
