## Hints

- How would a teammate eight hours away get unblocked without waiting for you to wake up?
- What belongs in writing, and when is a quick call better?
- How do you make your progress and availability visible without being micromanaged?

## Answer

Remote and async work rewards people who are **clear in writing, proactive and reliable**. Interviewers want to know you won't disappear or block others.

**Good practices to describe:**
- **Write things down:** clear PR descriptions, short design docs or RFCs for decisions, decision logs, and updated READMEs and runbooks. Documentation becomes the team's memory.
- **Async-first messages that are complete:** context, the question, what you've already tried, a deadline, and a proposed default ("If I don't hear back by Thursday, I'll go with option B"). Avoid a bare "hi" followed by waiting.
- **Use the right channel:** async for status and non-urgent questions. A **quick call** for conflicts, tricky debugging or ambiguous discussions, followed by a written summary.
- **Visibility:** regular short updates (standup notes, ticket status), small frequent PRs, and sharing blockers early.
- **Respect time zones and focus:** overlap hours for synchronous needs, clear availability in your calendar and status, and no expectation of instant replies.
- **Reliability:** do what you said, when you said, or say early that you can't.
- **Connection:** remote work can isolate people, so join pairing sessions and informal chats, and give positive feedback in public.

Back it with a concrete example from your own work, such as how you handed off a task across time zones or ran a decision through an RFC.

**What interviewers look for:** strong written communication, self-management, ownership, and empathy for teammates' time.

**Red flags:**
- Needing constant synchronous contact or supervision to make progress.
- Going silent for days, or hiding problems.
- Treating remote work purely as a perk without describing how you stay effective.
