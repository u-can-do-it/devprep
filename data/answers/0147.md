## Hints

- What is the STAR structure, and which part should take up most of your answer?
- How did you try to understand the other person's point of view?
- What changed afterwards — in the outcome and in your working relationship?

## Answer

This is a behavioral question: the interviewer wants evidence of how you handle friction, not proof that you've never had any.

**Use STAR:**

- **Situation:** short context — team, project, what the conflict was about (keep it about work: code reviews, priorities, ownership, communication style).
- **Task:** what was at stake and what your responsibility was.
- **Action (most of the answer):** what *you* did. Strong actions include: talking one-on-one rather than escalating publicly, asking questions to understand their concerns, separating the person from the problem, finding shared goals, proposing a compromise or an objective way to decide (data, a spike, a prototype), and involving a lead only when needed.
- **Result:** the concrete outcome, what improved in the relationship, and what you learned or would do differently.

**What interviewers look for:**

- Empathy and active listening.
- Ownership — acknowledging your own part.
- Calm, direct, respectful communication.
- Focus on the team and product outcome rather than "winning".

**Red flags:**

- "I've never had a conflict" — sounds evasive or inexperienced.
- Blaming the other person entirely or describing them negatively.
- Escalating to the manager as the first step.
- A story with no resolution or no lesson.
- Conflicts that reveal poor judgment (e.g. a personal feud).

**Tip:** prepare one or two stories in advance; a disagreement during code review or sprint planning works well for most levels.
