## Hints

- What in your current work already looks like this job?
- What do you find interesting about how models learn to write code?
- How much time can you realistically commit, and how do you keep quality up when working alone?

## Answer

Give a motivation that is **specific, believable and connected to your experience**. About 45–60 seconds.

**Good points to build on:**

- **You already do a version of this job.** You use AI coding tools every day and review everything they produce, so you know the typical mistakes (unsafe casts, race conditions, outdated APIs) and can explain why they're wrong.
- **Impact:** better training data means better code for many developers, including you and your team.
- **Learning:** reviewing many solutions to many problems sharpens your own TypeScript and review skills, and shows you how models are evaluated.
- **Fit:** you enjoy code review and technical writing, and you work well asynchronously and independently.
- **Availability:** state realistically how many hours per week you can commit and when.

**Example:**

> "I've been working with TypeScript and React for over six years, and for the last year or two AI tools have been part of my daily work. I review everything they generate, and I keep seeing the same kinds of mistakes: casts that hide wrong data, effects with race conditions, APIs from the wrong version. I'm good at spotting and explaining those, and this role turns that into direct improvements to the models. I also enjoy code review and mentoring, which is close to writing clear evaluations. I can commit around N hours a week, mostly evenings and weekends, and I work well on my own."

**Avoid:**

- "It's easy extra money": true or not, it signals low quality.
- Criticising your current employer.
- Vague enthusiasm ("AI is the future") with no link to your skills.
- Overpromising hours you can't keep; consistency matters more than volume.
