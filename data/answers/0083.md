## Hints

- Which join keeps rows from the left table even when there's no match on the right?
- What value appears in columns of the "missing" side?
- How would you find customers who have never placed an order?

## Answer

Given `customers` and `orders` (joined on `orders.customer_id = customers.id`):

- **INNER JOIN** – only rows with a match in **both** tables. Customers without orders and orders without customers are dropped.
- **LEFT (OUTER) JOIN** – **all rows from the left** table plus matching right rows; non-matching right columns are `NULL`.
- **RIGHT (OUTER) JOIN** – mirror of LEFT; rarely used since you can swap table order.
- **FULL (OUTER) JOIN** – all rows from both sides, `NULL`s where there's no match (not supported in MySQL; emulate with `UNION`).
- **CROSS JOIN** – Cartesian product: every row with every row.
- **Self join** – a table joined to itself, e.g. employees and their managers.

```sql
-- customers with their order count, including those with zero
SELECT c.id, c.name, COUNT(o.id) AS orders
FROM customers c
LEFT JOIN orders o ON o.customer_id = c.id
GROUP BY c.id, c.name;

-- anti-join: customers who never ordered
SELECT c.*
FROM customers c
LEFT JOIN orders o ON o.customer_id = c.id
WHERE o.id IS NULL;
```

Pitfalls:

- Putting a condition on the right table in `WHERE` instead of `ON` silently turns a LEFT JOIN into an INNER JOIN (`WHERE o.status = 'paid'` removes the `NULL` rows).
- Joining one-to-many relations multiplies rows, which inflates `SUM`/`COUNT` results.
- Join columns should be indexed (especially foreign keys).
