## Hints

- Which parts of your background are actually relevant to this specific role?
- How can you structure the answer so it flows from past to present to future?
- How long should this answer take — 30 seconds, 2 minutes, or 10?

## Answer

This is usually the opening question. The interviewer wants a short, relevant narrative — not your life story or a recital of your CV.

**Structure: Present → Past → Future (about 1.5–2 minutes)**

1. **Present:** your current role, the stack, and the scope of what you own. "I'm a full-stack developer at a SaaS company, working mostly with React, Next.js, and Node on our billing product."
2. **Past:** two or three highlights that explain how you got here and prove relevant skills — ideally with a concrete result ("led the migration to the App Router, cutting page load time by 40%").
3. **Future:** why this role is the logical next step, connected to what the company does.

**What interviewers look for:**

- Clear, concise communication.
- Relevance to the job description.
- Genuine motivation and some knowledge of the company.
- A few "hooks" they can ask follow-up questions about.

**For juniors:** focus on projects, internships, bootcamp/university work, and what you built and learned.

**Red flags:**

- Rambling for 5+ minutes or reading your CV line by line.
- Personal details unrelated to work dominating the answer.
- Negativity about current or past employers.
- No connection to the role you're interviewing for.

**Tip:** prepare and rehearse it, but don't memorize word-for-word — tailor the "past" highlights to each company.
