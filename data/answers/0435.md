## Hints

- What specifically about this company's product, engineering culture or stage attracts you?
- How does this role fit the direction you want to grow in?
- How can your answer about the future be honest and also aligned with what the role offers?

## Answer

Both questions test **motivation and fit**: will you be engaged here, and will you stay long enough for the investment in you to pay off?

**"Why do you want to work here?"** Be specific, which requires research:
- **Product or mission:** what they do and why it interests you (you've used it, or you care about the domain).
- **Engineering:** their stack, engineering blog, open-source work, how they ship (e.g. "you deploy many times a day with trunk-based development, which is how I like to work").
- **Role and stage:** what you can **contribute** (your skills meet their needs) and what you want to **learn** there.

A good formula: *something specific about them*, plus *how my experience helps with it*, plus *what I want to grow into here*.

**"Where do you see yourself in five years?"**
- Talk about **direction, not job titles**: deeper expertise (e.g. becoming the person who owns performance or architecture), broader scope (end-to-end ownership, leading projects), or moving toward a staff or management track if that's genuinely your aim.
- Link it to what this company can offer, and stay flexible: "I'd like to be leading technical decisions for a product area. Whether that's through a staff or a lead role, I'm open."

**What interviewers look for:** genuine interest, preparation, realistic ambition, and alignment between your goals and the role.

**Red flags:**
- Generic answers that would fit any company ("you're a great, innovative company").
- Only talking about money, perks or remote work.
- "I want your job" or "running my own startup" with no link to this role.
- No idea what the company actually does.
