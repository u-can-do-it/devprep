## Hints

- An attacker downloads your app from the store. What can they extract from it?
- Certificate pinning protects against which attacker, and what can it break?
- Which check tells your server that a request really comes from your unmodified app?

## Answer

> Start from the threat model: the attacker has your app binary, can run it on a rooted phone, and can inspect its traffic. So nothing shipped in the app is secret, and every rule must be enforced on the server. On the device you reduce risk: keep tokens in the Keychain or Keystore, use HTTPS only, add certificate pinning and app attestation when the risk justifies them, and treat deep links and WebView messages as untrusted input. Root detection and obfuscation slow an attacker down, they don't stop one.

**What the client can't protect**

- **Secrets in the bundle.** The JS bundle, Hermes bytecode, config files and `EXPO_PUBLIC_*` variables can all be extracted. Ship only publishable keys. A call that needs a secret key goes through your backend.
- **Business rules.** Prices, limits, roles and "is premium" checks in the app are UI only. The server decides.
- **Its own integrity.** A determined attacker can modify the app and remove any check.

**Data at rest**

- Tokens and keys in secure storage, never AsyncStorage (#681).
- Store as little as possible. Encrypt sensitive local databases, with the key in the Keystore.
- Exclude sensitive data from cloud backups.
- No tokens or personal data in logs, analytics or crash reports. Strip `console.log` from production builds.

**Data in transit**

- HTTPS only. Both platforms block plain HTTP by default; don't add exceptions.
- **Certificate pinning:** the app accepts only your server's public key, so a rogue or user-installed CA certificate can't intercept traffic.
  - Worth it for banking, health and similar apps.
  - Cost: a certificate change can lock every user out. Pin public keys, include backup pins, and plan the rotation.
  - Configure it natively (Android Network Security Config, iOS `NSPinnedDomains`) or with a library.
  - On a rooted device it can be bypassed with instrumentation tools. It raises the cost; it isn't absolute.

**Is this my real app? Attestation**

Play Integrity (Android) and App Attest (iOS) give the app a signed statement that your **server** verifies: the app is the genuine build, on a genuine device. This is the right tool against bots, modified apps and scripted abuse of your API. Combine it with rate limiting and normal authentication.

**Hardening (delay, not prevention)**

- **Root and jailbreak detection:** a signal for risk scoring. Easy to bypass, and it has false positives.
- **Obfuscation:** R8 for Kotlin and Java, bytecode instead of readable JS, optional JS obfuscators. Makes reverse engineering slower.
- **Screen protection:** `FLAG_SECURE` on Android blocks screenshots and screen recording of sensitive screens; hide content in the app switcher on iOS.

**Untrusted input**

- **Deep links:** validate parameters, and never perform an action directly from a link (#684).
- **WebViews:** restrict origins, validate messages, don't inject tokens (#690).
- **Push payloads and clipboard content:** the same rule.

**Auth**

System-browser OAuth with PKCE, short-lived access tokens, rotated refresh tokens, and biometrics tied to the stored secret (#697).

**Supply chain and updates**

- Every dependency runs with full app permissions, and native ones add compiled code. Pin versions, review new native dependencies, and watch for abandoned packages.
- Sign over-the-air updates, and protect who can publish them (#699).
- Protect signing keys and store accounts with MFA and limited access.

**How to know you've covered it**

Use the OWASP Mobile Application Security Verification Standard (MASVS) as the checklist, pick the level that matches the app's risk, and get a penetration test before a high-risk launch.

**Follow-up:** the product manager wants the app to call a paid third-party API directly "to save a backend endpoint". What do you answer? (The API key would ship in the app and be extracted, and anyone could spend your quota. Put a thin authenticated, rate-limited proxy on the backend, or use the provider's scheme for short-lived client tokens.)
