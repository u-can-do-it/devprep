## Hints

- Which of your strengths is most relevant to this specific role, and what evidence can you give?
- Is your weakness real, and are you actively doing something about it?
- Why is "I'm a perfectionist" a weak answer?

## Answer

The interviewer is testing **self-awareness, honesty and relevance**, not looking for perfection.

**Strengths: claim, evidence, relevance**
- Pick **1–2 strengths that matter for this role** (e.g. debugging complex production issues, turning vague requirements into a clear plan, making UIs accessible).
- Back each one with a **concrete example and result**: "I traced a memory leak in our Node API that had caused weekly restarts. Heap snapshots pointed to an unbounded cache, and after the fix restarts stopped."
- Connect it to their needs: "...which seems useful given you're scaling your backend."

**Weakness: real, with a plan and progress**
1. Name a **genuine but not role-critical** weakness (e.g. over-investing in polish before validating, hesitating to speak up in large meetings, limited cloud or infra experience as a frontend developer).
2. Show its impact honestly.
3. Explain **what you're doing about it** and what has improved: time-boxing, asking for early feedback, writing design notes before meetings, a course plus a side project.

**What interviewers look for:** self-reflection, a growth mindset, honesty, and examples rather than adjectives.

**Red flags:**
- Disguised strengths ("I work too hard", "I'm a perfectionist") come across as rehearsed and evasive.
- A weakness that is core to the job (e.g. "I don't like writing tests" for a senior role) with no mitigation.
- Lists of adjectives ("hard-working, passionate, team player") with no evidence.
- Blaming others or the past team for your weaknesses.
