## Hints

- Which story shows a real mistake, but one you can recover from convincingly?
- How much of the answer should describe the fix and the lessons, compared to the mistake itself?
- What did you change in the process so it couldn't happen again?

## Answer

The interviewer is checking self-awareness, honesty, ownership, and whether you learn and improve systems — not looking for a perfect track record.

**Structure (STAR + Learning):**

- **Situation/Task:** brief context.
- **The mistake:** state it plainly and own it ("I deployed a migration without testing it against production-sized data, and it locked a table for 10 minutes").
- **Action:** how you responded — raised it quickly, communicated with stakeholders, fixed or rolled back.
- **Result:** the impact and the recovery.
- **Learning and prevention:** what you changed — a checklist, a test, a CI check, a runbook, a blameless postmortem. This is the most important part.

**Good story choices:** a real technical or judgment mistake with moderate impact — a production bug, a missed estimate, under-communicating a delay, building the wrong thing because you didn't clarify requirements.

**What interviewers look for:**

- Genuine ownership without excessive self-criticism.
- Transparency — you raised the problem instead of hiding it.
- Systemic thinking — fixing the process, not just the instance.
- For senior candidates: how you helped the team learn from it.

**Red flags:**

- A disguised strength ("I work too hard").
- Blaming others, tools, or circumstances.
- A catastrophic mistake with no meaningful lessons.
- Claiming you can't think of any failure.
