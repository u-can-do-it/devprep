## Hints

- Can you explain why this achievement was hard, not just what you built?
- What exactly was *your* contribution, as opposed to the team's?
- Can you measure the outcome: users, performance, money, time saved?

## Answer

This question reveals what you value, how you work, and the scope you can handle. Pick an achievement that is **relevant to the role** and shows the level you're applying for.

**Structure (STAR, plus reflection):**
- **Situation:** context in 1–2 sentences: product, team, why it mattered.
- **Task:** the goal and what made it **hard** (scale, ambiguity, legacy code, deadline, unfamiliar technology).
- **Action:** most of your time goes here. Describe **your** decisions and trade-offs using "I", while crediting the team. Explain *why* you chose an approach and what alternatives you rejected.
- **Result:** a measurable outcome (e.g. "p95 latency from 1.2 s to 300 ms", "onboarding time halved", "zero-downtime migration of 40M rows").
- **Reflection:** what you learned or would do differently. This shows maturity.

**Adjust by level:**
- Junior: shipping a non-trivial feature end to end, learning fast, a great bug hunt.
- Mid: owning a feature or service, cross-team work, measurable impact.
- Senior and above: technical leadership, influencing direction, multiplying others, business impact.

**What interviewers look for:** ownership, technical depth, impact awareness, the ability to explain complex work clearly, and pride grounded in results. Expect follow-ups probing the details, so pick something you know deeply.

**Red flags:**
- Vague answers ("I built a microservices platform") with no personal role or outcome.
- Taking credit for team work, or the opposite, only ever saying "we".
- An achievement irrelevant to software or years out of date when recent ones exist.
- Being unable to answer technical follow-ups about your own project.
